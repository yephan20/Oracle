When temperatures drop, finding the best thermostat setting for winter becomes a balancing act between comfort and energy savings. Cranking up the heat might feel great in the moment, but it can send your energy bills soaring. On the other hand, keeping your home too cool can make for some chilly evenings.

The Ideal Temperature Range

For most households, the sweet spot is around 68°F (20°C) while you’re awake and at home. This temperature keeps your living space comfortable without overworking your heating system.

When you’re asleep or away, lowering your thermostat by 7–10 degrees can significantly reduce energy use. Many energy experts suggest setting it closer to 60–62°F (15–17°C) overnight for optimal savings.

Why Small Adjustments Make a Big Difference

It might not seem like much, but even lowering your thermostat by 1 degree can reduce your heating bill by about 1–3% over time. Those small changes add up quickly over the course of a winter season.

The key is consistency. Instead of constantly adjusting the temperature throughout the day, aim for a steady schedule that aligns with your routine.

Nighttime Settings for Better Sleep

Sleeping in a slightly cooler room isn’t just good for your wallet—it’s better for your sleep. Temperatures between 60–67°F (15–19°C) are often recommended for optimal rest.

Pairing a cooler room with warm blankets can create a cozy sleep environment without relying heavily on your heating system.

Adjusting for Your Home and Lifestyle

There’s no one-size-fits-all answer. The best setting depends on factors like:

      • Your home’s insulation
      • Outdoor temperatures
      • Personal comfort preferences
      • The age and efficiency of your heating system

If your home tends to lose heat quickly, you might need to set the thermostat slightly higher—but improving insulation or sealing drafts can often solve that problem more efficiently.

Tips to Stay Warm Without Raising the Thermostat

Before turning up the heat, try a few simple strategies:

      • Use thick curtains to keep cold air out
      • Seal gaps around windows and doors
      • Layer up with warm clothing
      • Use area rugs to reduce heat loss through floors
      • Let sunlight in during the day for natural warmth

These small changes can make your home feel noticeably warmer without increasing energy usage.

The best thermostat setting in winter strikes a balance between comfort and efficiency. For most people, that means around 68°F during the day and cooler temperatures at night or when away.
